# RideSentinel, "POV: the 2 a.m. offer" (EN · FR · PT · AR · ZH): treatment and script

**Logline.** A driver at a red light at 2 a.m. gets an offer and panics through the maths out loud. The light turns green, horns blare,
and the offer is gone. Then the same driver with RideSentinel: one glance, PASS, TAKE, eyes on the road.

**Why this engages.**
- A "POV:" meme hook in the first second.
- A face that reacts: eyebrows, eyes darting, sweat, a jump at the horn.
- A relatable stress (two countdowns at once: the offer and the red light).
- A comic payoff ("…and it's gone").
- Quick cuts every 1–2 s.
- Word-by-word karaoke captions, so it works muted.
- A tight 2 s end card.

**Safety by design.** The panic happens **stopped at a red light**, never while moving. In act 2 the car is moving, so he gets **one glance**
and his eyes go straight back up. The phone stays mounted.

**Look.** The same brand world (navy, amber, teal, rose, Barlow Condensed and IBM Plex), now with a **new expressive character** seen
from the front (the camera sits on the dashboard):
- a flat 2D cartoon: big eyes with pupils that move, eyebrows, mouth shapes (smile, "O", grimace, grin), a sweat drop, hands on the wheel;
- the **traffic light's colour washes over his face**: red while he calculates, then a hard green when the light changes;
- cuts to a **phone insert** (the same offer card and Rate Dial as the other films);
- big, centred captions, with the spoken word lit in amber.

**Sound.** The ElevenLabs voiceover is his inner monologue: fast and anxious in act 1, calm and confident in act 2. Under it:
- the generated score: a ticking clock in act 1, a car horn + a whoosh on the green light, a sad trombone-like "bwomp" on "it's gone";
- the beat drops on "Not anymore" / "Plus maintenant";
- a chime on TAKE.

**Structure** (~24 s; the picture is timed to the measured voice lines in each language).

## Build notes
- **Files:**
  - `pov-{en,fr,pt,ar,zh}.html` + `pov.js` are the film. `pov.js` is assembled from `film.js` (text, phone, dial, end card, score helpers)
    + `pov-scene.part.js` + `pov-audio.part.js`;
  - the text is `strings.py` → `strings.js` (`pov` block) and `source/pov-<lang>/script.txt`.
- **Timing comes from the voice.** `align-<lang>.js` (measured line and word times + a 100 Hz loudness envelope) is made by
  `../tools/make_align.py` from `voice.align.json`.
  - Until it exists, the film estimates timings from the script, so every cut, gag and caption is already in place.
  - The cut list, the green light, the horns, "gone", PASS and TAKE, and the end card are all derived from line and word times.
- **The driver:** a front-facing cartoon built from parts:
  - expression parameters per beat (brows, worry, gaze, lids, pop, mouth shape, sweat, jump, slump);
  - the mouth opens with the voice's loudness;
  - drawn on a layer and lit by the traffic light (red → green), the phone glow, and passing street lamps in act 2.
- **Bugs found while inspecting:**
  - The horn shake uncovered an unpainted strip of the previous frame. Every frame now starts with a full fill.
  - The first draw of large text rendered slightly differently from later ones (glyph cache). The page now does one warm-up pass before
    `ready`.
  - All canvas state is reset at the start of every frame.
- **Verified:**
  - `verify.mjs` passes for all five languages (EN also at `--ss 2`).
  - A dry run with a synthetic voice exercised the whole voice path (align → embed → mix → render).
  - I read contact sheets for EN, FR, AR and ZH.
